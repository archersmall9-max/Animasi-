# Upload sheet — Orange the earless cat / ice cream

Everything below is copy-paste ready. Nothing here needs editing except where
marked.

---

## 1. Which file

**Upload `exports/CatIceCream_Shorts_NOMUSIC.mp4`** and add a track from
YouTube's own audio picker in the Shorts editor.

The music on the original is a mastered library track, almost certainly
licensed through Instagram. Carrying it across invites a Content ID claim,
which at best moves the revenue to the rights holder and at worst blocks the
video in some countries. Swapping in YouTube's own audio costs nothing, is
pre-cleared, and the Shorts feed favours it.

`CatIceCream_Shorts_MASTER.mp4` keeps the original music. Use it only if you
know the track is cleared.

search — "cat with no ears" — sits in the first four words.

```text
Orange is a ginger cat with no ears. They were removed completely after an ear tumour, and despite how he looks he can still hear perfectly fine.

Original video by Orange the earless cat — @earlesspotato on Instagram and TikTok. All credit to them; go and follow the original account.

Here he gets an ice cream bar held right under his chin. One careful sniff, one lick, and then the most unbothered stare you will see today. The surgery left him with a round, potato-shaped head, which is why people online call him the earless hachimi.

Worth saying, since it comes up every time: most adult cats are lactose intolerant, so ice cream is not a treat to give them. A single lick of plain ice cream is unlikely to hurt a healthy cat, but dairy, sugar and fat all sit badly with an obligate carnivore, and anything with chocolate or a sugar-free sweetener is genuinely dangerous. A chilled spoonful of their own wet food does the same job safely.

Follow @Lowk67Tuff for more.

#earlesscat #orangecat #cat #hachimi #cutecat
```

The credit is the second paragraph on purpose — it is above the fold, so it is
visible without anyone tapping "more".

## 5. Pinned comment — post this yourself, immediately after publishing

```text
Credit where it's due: this is Orange, filmed by @earlesspotato on Instagram and TikTok. His ears were removed after an ear tumour and he hears perfectly fine. Go follow the original account.
```

A pinned comment is the strongest credit placement available without putting
text on the video itself. It sits at the top of the comment section, it is the
first thing anyone looking for the source will see, and it is what a rights
holder checks before deciding whether to file a complaint rather than send a
message.

---

## 6. Upload settings

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | **Not made for kids** | General-audience pet content. Marking it made-for-kids disables comments, pinned comments and notifications, and guts reach. |
| Comments | **On** | The pinned credit needs somewhere to live, and comment rate is a ranking signal. |
| Visibility | Public | — |
| Language | English | Matches the metadata. |
| Altered content disclosure | Not required | No synthetic or AI-generated footage. |

Do not add hashtags to the title. The five in the description already classify
the video, and YouTube renders the first three above the title anyway.

## 7. The thing that would actually settle this

You have decided credit is enough, and that is your call to make. For what it
costs, this is still worth sending to @earlesspotato before you publish:

```text
Hi! I edited one of your videos of Orange (the ice cream one) into a vertical cut for YouTube Shorts, credited to you in the description and in a pinned comment. Happy to take it down any time if you'd rather I didn't post it — just say the word. Either way, he's a great cat.
```

Thirty seconds, and a silent risk turns into either a yes or a clear no. Most
pet accounts say yes, because it sends them followers.
